GANNI — Bridging the Viral Demand Gap

CategoryMarketing
Project TypeGraduate capstone
CollaborationTeam project
Focus Areas
Brand StrategySupply Chain StrategyDemand SensingSustainability

Overview

GANNI faces a recurring challenge: when a product goes viral, the brand lacks the supply-chain agility to meet demand before the moment passes. This team project proposes an AI-supported demand-sensing system paired with a nearshore production model to close that gap. By monitoring social signals in near-real time and shifting a portion of production to regional partners, the strategy offers a path to faster inventory response, reduced overstock, and more sustainable fulfillment — without sacrificing the brand's Scandinavian design ethos. The estimated revenue recovery potential from avoided stockouts is modeled at up to $2.9M.

Challenge

GANNI consistently loses revenue when viral products sell out faster than the global supply chain can replenish, forcing markdowns on overstock elsewhere in the line.

Contribution

Developed the strategic framework, demand-signal mapping, and nearshore sourcing rationale as part of a graduate team. Contributed to slide design and the executive presentation.

Deliverables

Strategic recommendations deck, demand-sensing framework, nearshore sourcing model, financial impact analysis (modeled estimates only), executive summary.

Context

GANNI has achieved outsized cultural visibility for a mid-luxury brand, driven by influencer adoption and a strong social community. That visibility creates a recurring business problem: products go viral faster than global supply chains can respond.

Market Insight

Consumer interest in fashion today peaks and decays on the timeline of a social feed — often days or weeks. Traditional seasonal forecasting models were not designed for this cadence. Brands that cannot respond within that window leave revenue on the table and risk appearing out-of-touch with the community that elevated them.

Strategic Opportunity

Two systems working in parallel can close the gap: (1) an AI-powered demand-sensing layer that monitors social velocity to flag emerging product interest before it peaks, and (2) a nearshore production model that keeps a portion of manufacturing within shorter lead-time distance of key markets.

Recommendation

The team recommends a phased adoption beginning with demand-signal monitoring tools integrated into existing planning workflows, followed by a pilot nearshore supplier program. The financial model estimates up to $2.9M in recoverable revenue from avoided stockouts — all figures are modeled projections, not reported results.

Key Takeaway

This project demonstrates Riya's ability to move from consumer insight to strategic recommendation — developing a complete, channel-literate marketing strategy rooted in brand understanding and audience empathy.
